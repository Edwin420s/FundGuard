# Omega FundGuard --- Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Baseline specification\
**Product:** Omega FundGuard\
**Primary user:** Hackathon organizers\
**Initial settlement network:** Stellar testnet\
**Agent framework:** SingularityNET Omega / Hyperon / MeTTa\
**Last updated:** 2026-10-23

## 1. Executive summary

Omega FundGuard is an AI-agent-coordinated payout management platform
for hackathons, accelerators, grant programs, competitions, and other
organizations distributing funds. It coordinates funding policies,
eligibility verification, award calculation, approvals, settlement
execution, transaction verification, reconciliation, and audit
reporting.

Omega is responsible for interpreting requests, retrieving context,
selecting tools, coordinating multi-step workflows, and explaining
outcomes. It is not the financial system of record and cannot bypass
deterministic controls or human authorization. Deterministic application
services enforce policy, monetary arithmetic, permissions, state
transitions, idempotency, and settlement invariants.

The initial integration is Stellar testnet, with verifiable transaction
evidence and a structured application audit log. The product
architecture must support multi-tenancy, richer governance, Soroban
where justified, additional settlement adapters, analytics, and
production operations without requiring a redesign of the core domain.

## 2. Product vision

Provide a transparent and auditable coordination layer that helps
organizations move from funding rules and verified award recipients to
approved, reconciled disbursements.

### 2.1 Product principles

1.  **Human accountability:** humans with appropriate permissions
    approve financial actions.
2.  **Deterministic money logic:** amounts, limits, reserves, and
    balances are calculated by tested code, not generated by an LLM.
3.  **Evidence-grounded agent behavior:** explanations must cite
    application records, policy versions, tool results, or transaction
    evidence.
4.  **Traceability:** every material action has an actor, timestamp,
    correlation identifier, and relevant evidence.
5.  **Network abstraction:** business workflows do not depend on a
    single chain's transaction format.
6.  **Secure by default:** the agent does not receive signing secrets
    and cannot directly bypass the execution service.
7.  **Incremental completeness:** implementation is staged, but the
    product design includes the full capability set.
8.  **Honest status:** submitted, confirmed, failed, and unresolved are
    distinct states.

## 3. Problem statement

Organizations often coordinate prize distributions with spreadsheets,
messaging tools, manual eligibility checks, and disconnected payment
records. This creates risks of opaque allocation, inconsistent policy
application, duplicate requests, untracked failed transfers, unclear
reserves, and costly reconciliation.

FundGuard provides a controlled workflow that records how an allocation
was calculated, who approved it, what was submitted, what the settlement
network confirmed, and how remaining obligations and balances were
reconciled.

## 4. Goals and non-goals

### 4.1 Goals

-   Configure versioned funding policies and eligibility rules.
-   Register recipients and capture evidence for eligibility decisions.
-   Calculate deterministic award allocations and reserve amounts.
-   Create payout batches containing multiple independently tracked
    payouts.
-   Require approval bound to the exact version/hash of a batch.
-   Execute authorized Stellar testnet transfers and verify transaction
    results.
-   Prevent unintended duplicate execution and unsafe blind retries.
-   Preserve structured audit events and references to network receipts.
-   Support partial batch success, failure handling, and reconciliation.
-   Provide an organizer dashboard, API, and reports.
-   Integrate Omega for real tool orchestration, context retrieval, and
    explainable workflows.
-   Design for multiple organizations, configurable roles, risk
    controls, and future settlement adapters.

### 4.2 Non-goals for the initial network deployment

These are not permanent product exclusions; they are not enabled in the
initial testnet release: - Transfers of live money or production mainnet
execution. - Claims of production regulatory compliance without
jurisdiction-specific review. - An unverified or invented cross-chain
transfer. - Treating an LLM response as proof of a payment or
eligibility. - Storing private recipient data or signing secrets on a
public blockchain.

## 5. Personas

### 5.1 Competition organizer

Creates competitions, configures funding rules, registers winners,
prepares payout batches, and monitors completion.

### 5.2 Finance approver

Reviews allocations, reserves, recipients, fees, policy compliance, and
the exact batch hash before approving.

### 5.3 Operations administrator

Manages organization members, permissions, integrations, incidents,
reconciliation, and exports.

### 5.4 Recipient

Views their own payout status and applicable receipt information,
subject to the competition's privacy and access settings.

### 5.5 Auditor or reviewer

Reads permitted audit records and verifies the link between proposal,
approval, transaction, and reconciliation.

## 6. Core user journeys

1.  **Create a competition:** organizer creates a workspace, funding
    configuration, and policy version.
2.  **Register winners:** teams and recipient addresses are entered;
    eligibility evidence is attached or referenced.
3.  **Prepare batch:** organizer asks Omega to prepare a payout; Omega
    retrieves the policy and calls eligibility and allocation tools.
4.  **Review proposal:** system displays recipients, amounts, asset,
    fees if available, reserve, excluded teams, assumptions, and
    validation results.
5.  **Approve:** authorized approver approves the exact canonical batch
    hash.
6.  **Execute:** payout service revalidates authorization and
    transaction intent, then submits an authorized transfer.
7.  **Verify:** service independently retrieves transaction/payment
    evidence and checks it against expected details.
8.  **Reconcile:** the system updates paid, outstanding, reserved, and
    unresolved amounts.
9.  **Audit/export:** authorized user filters history and exports
    evidence.
10. **Recover:** unresolved outcomes are reconciled before a retry or
    manual intervention.

## 7. Functional requirements

Priority labels: **MUST** = required for the product's core integrity;
**SHOULD** = expected in the complete platform; **COULD** = optional
enhancement.

### FR-01 --- Organization and tenancy

-   MUST associate organization-owned records with an organization
    identifier.
-   MUST enforce tenant isolation in service authorization and database
    access patterns.
-   MUST support organization membership and role assignments.
-   SHOULD support invitations, organization lifecycle, and configurable
    retention.
-   MUST prevent users from accessing another organization's records
    without explicit authorization.

### FR-02 --- Competition management

-   MUST support draft, active, closed, and archived competition states.
-   MUST associate a competition with an organization and funding policy
    versions.
-   SHOULD support reusable competition templates and configurable
    metadata.

### FR-03 --- Funding sources and treasury

-   MUST represent a funding source, network, asset, and
    available-balance snapshot.
-   MUST distinguish available, reserved, paid, outstanding, and
    unresolved amounts.
-   MUST prevent concurrent batches from reserving more than the
    permitted available funds.
-   SHOULD support multiple funding sources and treasury reconciliation.
-   MUST record the provenance and timestamp of balance snapshots.

### FR-04 --- Policy management

-   MUST support versioned structured policies.
-   MUST validate policy schema, limits, thresholds, and award rules
    before activation.
-   MUST preserve the policy version referenced by each batch.
-   MUST prevent an edit from silently changing an approved batch.
-   SHOULD provide policy comparison and simulation.
-   SHOULD support fixed awards, ranked awards, proportional release,
    staged release, per-recipient caps, and reserve rules.

### FR-05 --- Recipient and eligibility management

-   MUST maintain stable recipient/team identifiers.
-   MUST validate required recipient fields and settlement address
    format.
-   MUST record eligibility status, evidence references, reviewer, and
    timestamp.
-   MUST represent missing, conflicting, rejected, expired, and verified
    evidence distinctly.
-   MUST not let the agent invent missing addresses or evidence.
-   SHOULD support recipient self-service views and configurable
    identity checks.

### FR-06 --- Allocation and validation

-   MUST use deterministic code for monetary arithmetic.
-   MUST reject negative or invalid amounts.
-   MUST enforce release caps, available-balance constraints,
    per-recipient limits, and configured policy rules.
-   MUST make calculations reproducible from stored inputs and policy
    version.
-   MUST detect duplicate recipients or repeated award obligations
    according to configured policy.
-   MUST produce machine-readable validation findings and human-readable
    explanations.

### FR-07 --- Payout batches

-   MUST support multiple payouts per batch.
-   MUST track each payout independently.
-   MUST support partial success without erasing successful outcomes.
-   MUST create a canonical representation and cryptographic hash of the
    approved batch intent.
-   MUST bind approvals to that hash.
-   MUST invalidate approval if any material field changes.
-   SHOULD support scheduled, staged, or recurring payouts with explicit
    policy controls.

### FR-08 --- Approval and governance

-   MUST authenticate the approver.
-   MUST verify the approver's organization membership and permission.
-   MUST bind approval to the exact batch hash and applicable policy
    version.
-   MUST support approve, reject, and request-amendment outcomes.
-   MUST require reapproval after material changes.
-   SHOULD support multiple approvers, amount thresholds, expiry,
    revocation before execution, and separation of duties.
-   MUST not treat a natural-language statement as authorization unless
    it is captured through an authenticated approval flow.

### FR-09 --- Settlement execution

-   MUST initially support Stellar testnet payout execution.
-   MUST validate network, asset, recipient, amount, authorization, and
    available funds before submission.
-   MUST isolate signing secrets from the Omega agent and ordinary
    application logs.
-   MUST record submission attempts and network references.
-   MUST independently verify transaction and payment-operation
    evidence.
-   MUST distinguish prepared, submitted, confirmed, failed, and
    unknown/unresolved outcomes.
-   MUST not report a transaction as confirmed solely because submission
    was requested or returned a hash.
-   SHOULD provide settlement adapters behind a common interface.
-   MUST keep mainnet execution disabled until production release gates
    pass.

### FR-10 --- Idempotency and concurrency

-   MUST require an idempotency key for execution requests.
-   MUST enforce idempotency with a database uniqueness constraint.
-   MUST use transactional locking or equivalent concurrency control for
    batch execution and treasury reservations.
-   MUST reconcile an unknown submission outcome before retrying.
-   MUST ensure retries do not create a second successful payment for
    the same obligation.
-   MUST preserve each attempt and its result.

### FR-11 --- Reconciliation and recovery

-   MUST compare expected application state with independently retrieved
    settlement evidence.
-   MUST support unresolved transaction investigations.
-   MUST record discrepancies and recovery decisions.
-   MUST support partial batch completion.
-   MUST not blindly retry a payment with uncertain outcome.
-   SHOULD support manual review queues and scheduled reconciliation
    jobs.

### FR-12 --- Audit and evidence

-   MUST record material policy, eligibility, proposal, approval,
    submission, verification, and reconciliation events.
-   MUST include event ID, timestamp, actor, entity, correlation/request
    ID, and evidence references.
-   MUST link payout records to verified chain transaction references.
-   MUST provide authorized search and filtering.
-   SHOULD support CSV/JSON exports and integrity verification.
-   SHOULD support hash-chained events and periodic on-chain
    commitments.
-   MUST not claim that an audit hash proves the truth of the original
    event.
-   MUST keep sensitive personal data off public chains.

### FR-13 --- Omega agent

-   MUST use Omega as an actual reasoning and tool-orchestration
    component, not merely as a chatbot wrapper.
-   MUST expose constrained, schema-validated application tools.
-   MUST ground explanations in tool results and authoritative records.
-   MUST stop or request clarification when critical data is missing or
    policy conflicts exist.
-   MUST not access signing secrets or directly bypass execution
    controls.
-   MUST preserve agent run and tool-call evidence subject to privacy
    and retention rules.
-   SHOULD support persistent contextual memory with policy and
    permission checks.
-   SHOULD provide repeatable evaluation suites for tool selection,
    policy adherence, and safe stopping.

### FR-14 --- Risk and compliance

-   SHOULD support configurable risk rules, identity checks, sanctions
    screening where applicable, case management, and manual review.
-   MUST distinguish a risk alert from a verified finding.
-   MUST apply jurisdiction and provider limitations explicitly.
-   MUST record decisions and evidence for risk-related holds.
-   MUST not claim legal compliance merely because a screening tool is
    integrated.

### FR-15 --- Analytics and reporting

-   MUST report batch totals and payout statuses.
-   SHOULD report fund utilization, reserves, fees, completion times,
    failure rates, unresolved amounts, and reconciliation differences.
-   SHOULD support historical batch and policy comparisons.
-   MUST enforce authorization and privacy controls on exports.

### FR-16 --- Developer platform and integrations

-   MUST publish versioned API contracts.
-   SHOULD support signed webhooks with replay protection.
-   SHOULD provide sandbox data, API documentation, health checks, and
    integration diagnostics.
-   MUST keep provider credentials and signing secrets out of client
    bundles and logs.

## 8. Non-functional requirements

### NFR-01 --- Security

Authentication and authorization are enforced server-side. All
high-impact actions require explicit permission. Secrets are stored in
an appropriate secret store or protected runtime configuration.
Dependencies and containers are scanned. Sensitive actions are audited.

### NFR-02 --- Tenant isolation

Every organization-scoped query and mutation must be authorized against
the active organization. Automated tests must attempt cross-tenant reads
and writes and verify denial.

### NFR-03 --- Financial correctness

Money is represented using integer base units or exact decimal types,
never binary floating-point for authoritative calculations. Allocation
results must be reproducible. Database constraints protect uniqueness
and referential integrity.

### NFR-04 --- Reliability

The system must tolerate duplicate API requests, worker restarts,
delayed network responses, and partial batch completion. Unknown
outcomes are reconciled before retries.

### NFR-05 --- Auditability

Every material workflow transition has a traceable event. Audit evidence
is exportable and integrity-checkable. The system retains sufficient
context to reconstruct the approved intent and compare it to settlement
evidence.

### NFR-06 --- Performance

Initial targets, to be validated by tests: - Typical read API p95 under
500 ms excluding external network calls. - Deterministic validation of a
batch of 100 recipients under 2 seconds on the reference development
environment. - Dashboard views paginate large audit histories. -
External network operations use explicit timeouts and bounded retries.

These are initial engineering targets, not measured claims.

### NFR-07 --- Availability and recoverability

-   Background jobs are safe to resume.
-   Database backups and restore procedures are documented and tested.
-   Reconciliation can resume after service restart.
-   Monitoring detects stuck payout states and repeated failures.

### NFR-08 --- Maintainability

Domain logic is separated from HTTP, agent, and network adapters. Public
APIs are versioned. Database changes use migrations. Tests cover
invariants and state transitions.

### NFR-09 --- Privacy

Collect only necessary personal data. Access is scoped by organization
and role. Public-chain data excludes sensitive personal information.
Retention and export behavior are documented.

### NFR-10 --- Observability

Use structured logs, correlation IDs, metrics for payout states, and
traces across API, agent, worker, and settlement calls. Never log
secrets or full sensitive identity documents.

### NFR-11 --- Agent safety and reproducibility

Agent behavior is evaluated against a versioned test set. Tool
permissions are enforced outside the model. Important outputs include
references to the records and tools supporting the explanation.

### NFR-12 --- Accessibility and usability

Core organizer workflows should be keyboard accessible, communicate
validation errors clearly, and distinguish proposed, approved,
submitted, confirmed, failed, and unresolved states without relying on
color alone.

## 9. Product metrics

Track: - Percentage of payouts with complete approval and receipt
evidence. - Duplicate payment prevention test pass rate. - Allocation
calculation correctness. - Reconciliation discrepancy count and age. -
Percentage of agent runs that use the appropriate tools. - Rate of
unsafe agent actions blocked by policy controls. - Median time from
proposal to approval and from approval to confirmation. - Number and age
of unresolved payouts. - Tenant-isolation test pass rate.

## 10. Product assumptions and open decisions

1.  Initial live integration uses Stellar testnet and test assets only.
2.  XLM or another explicitly configured supported asset is selected per
    test scenario; illustrative fiat award values are not automatically
    converted to token amounts.
3.  Funding policies are approved by an authorized human before use.
4.  Production mainnet and external payment-provider integrations
    require separate release approvals.
5.  Legal, custody, tax, and compliance obligations depend on
    jurisdiction and operating model and require qualified review.
6.  Exact Omega and Raven APIs must be verified against their current
    repositories and supported integration contracts.
7.  A custom Soroban contract is built only when its trust guarantees
    are defined and testable.

## 11. Definition of done

A release is complete when its requirements are implemented, tests pass,
documentation is updated, known limitations are documented, and evidence
is captured. A testnet-complete release must demonstrate a verified
testnet payment, exact-batch approval, structured audit history,
duplicate protection, and safe handling of an unresolved or failed
transaction.
